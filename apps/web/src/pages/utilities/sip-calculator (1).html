What is SIP investing?

SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) is a method of investing a fixed amount regularly — typically monthly — into a mutual fund scheme. It is the most popular investment approach in India for building long-term wealth, with over 7 crore active SIP accounts as of 2024. The monthly SIP amount is automatically deducted from your bank account and invested in your chosen fund at the prevailing Net Asset Value (NAV) on the specified date.

The core principle of SIP is rupee cost averaging — by investing the same amount every month regardless of market conditions, you automatically buy more units when prices are low and fewer units when prices are high. Over a long period, this averaging effect reduces the impact of market volatility and produces better returns than trying to time the market. SIP also enforces financial discipline by automating the savings process.

The power of compounding in SIP

The most powerful aspect of long-term SIP investing is the compounding effect. Compounding means earning returns not just on your invested capital but also on the returns generated previously. The longer you stay invested, the more dramatically compounding works in your favour. A ₹10,000 monthly SIP at 12% annual returns for 10 years produces a maturity value of approximately ₹23 lakhs on a total investment of ₹12 lakhs — nearly double your investment. The same SIP continued for 20 years produces approximately ₹99 lakhs on a total investment of ₹24 lakhs — more than four times the investment. This exponential growth is entirely due to compounding working over time.

Understanding SIP returns

SIP returns are typically expressed as CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate) or XIRR (Extended Internal Rate of Return). XIRR is the more accurate metric for SIPs because it accounts for the timing of each cash flow — each monthly instalment is invested at a different time. Historically, diversified equity mutual funds in India have delivered 12–15% CAGR over long periods (10+ years), though past performance is not a guarantee of future returns. This calculator uses a constant annual return rate for simplicity; actual returns will vary based on market performance.

SIP vs lump sum investment

A lump sum investment puts all your capital to work immediately, which is advantageous in rising markets. SIP spreads your investment over time, which reduces risk in volatile or declining markets. For most individual investors, SIP is preferable because it removes the need to time the market, is compatible with regular income, and benefits from rupee cost averaging. For investors with a large corpus available (such as after a bonus or inheritance), a combination of both — a lump sum plus a monthly SIP — is often recommended by financial advisors.

Step-up SIP

A Step-up SIP (also called a Top-up SIP) allows you to increase your monthly SIP amount annually — typically by 10–15% per year, aligned with salary increments. Step-up SIPs significantly outperform flat SIPs because the increasing investment amount amplifies the compounding effect over time. This calculator assumes a constant monthly investment; for step-up SIP calculations, increase the monthly amount by your expected annual increment and recalculate.

What is the minimum SIP amount? +
Most mutual fund companies in India accept SIP amounts starting from ₹500 per month. Some funds, particularly ELSS (tax-saving) funds, may have a minimum of ₹500 or ₹1,000. Direct plans (bought directly from the fund house without a broker) have the same minimums and lower expense ratios than regular plans.
Can I stop or change my SIP amount? +
Yes. Most mutual funds allow you to pause, stop, or change your SIP amount with reasonable notice (typically 10–15 business days before the next deduction date). Stopping a SIP does not mean you need to redeem your investment — your accumulated units remain invested and continue to earn returns.
Is SIP investment safe? +
SIPs invest in market-linked instruments (mutual funds) and are subject to market risk. The value of your investment can go down as well as up. However, the risk is significantly reduced over longer investment horizons (5+ years) due to rupee cost averaging and the general upward trend of equity markets over time. SEBI regulates mutual funds in India, providing investor protection.
What is an ELSS and how does it differ from regular SIP? +
ELSS (Equity Linked Savings Scheme) is a type of mutual fund that qualifies for tax deduction under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act (up to ₹1.5 lakh per year). ELSS funds have a mandatory 3-year lock-in period per SIP instalment. Other mutual fund SIPs have no lock-in period (except for specific schemes). ELSS is popular for combining tax saving with equity market exposure.
How are SIP returns taxed in India? +
For equity mutual funds held for more than 1 year, returns are classified as Long Term Capital Gains (LTCG) and taxed at 10% above ₹1 lakh per year. For holdings under 1 year, Short Term Capital Gains (STCG) are taxed at 15%. For debt funds, the holding period for LTCG is 3 years, with tax at 20% with indexation benefit. Each SIP instalment is treated as a separate investment for tax purposes.
