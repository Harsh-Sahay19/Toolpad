What this IBAN generator does

The generator creates IBANs (International Bank Account Numbers) for the United Kingdom, Germany and France that pass the MOD-97 check. The bank identifiers come from a short built-in list, but the account numbers are random. The numbers are well formed, and they do not belong to any real account.

How to use it

  1. Choose a country, and how many IBANs you need.
  2. Click Generate, then copy the list or download the CSV.

How an IBAN is built

  • Country code (2 letters): for example GB, DE or FR.
  • Check digits (2 digits): calculated from the rest of the number using the MOD-97 method.
  • Basic bank account number (BBAN): the national bank code, branch code and account number. Its length and format depend on the country (22 characters for GB and DE, 27 for FR).

To check an IBAN by hand, move the first four characters to the end, replace each letter with two digits (A = 10 through Z = 35), and take the result modulo 97. A valid IBAN gives a remainder of 1. This is the same check that banks and payment forms use to catch typing errors.

What a valid check digit does and does not prove

A valid IBAN is correctly formed. It does not show that the account exists, is open, belongs to a particular person, or can receive money. Only the bank can confirm an account.

Limits and responsible use

  • Use generated IBANs only in test environments, for example to check that a payment form accepts well-formed input.
  • Never use them for real transfers, direct debits or invoices.
  • Do not try to find or use a real person's account details to test a system. Test with generated values instead.
