What this IMEI generator does

The generator produces 15-digit IMEI numbers that pass the Luhn check, so they are structurally valid. You can pick a brand (Apple, Samsung, Google Pixel, OnePlus, Xiaomi, Huawei, Nokia or Motorola) to start each number with that brand's TAC prefix, or leave the selector on random. The Validate tab checks any IMEI you paste, one at a time or in bulk, for length, digits and the Luhn check digit.

Generated IMEIs are random in their serial part. They are for software testing only: a device-ID field, a validation rule, a database column or a billing test that needs 15-digit input.

How to use it

  1. Choose a brand, or leave Any brand (random) selected.
  2. Choose how many IMEIs you need, from 5 to 100.
  3. Click Generate, then copy the list or download it as a CSV with the IMEI, brand and TAC columns.
  4. To check existing values, switch to the Validate tab and paste one IMEI, or several separated by new lines.

How an IMEI is built

An IMEI (International Mobile Equipment Identity) has 15 digits in three parts:

  • TAC (first 8 digits): the Type Allocation Code. It identifies the manufacturer and model family. The GSMA assigns these codes.
  • Serial number (next 6 digits): assigned by the manufacturer to each unit within a model.
  • Check digit (last digit): calculated with the Luhn algorithm. The generator sets this digit so the whole number passes the Luhn check.

The Luhn check catches most single-digit typing mistakes. Passing it means the number is well formed. It does not show that the number belongs to a real device or that it is registered with a network.

Limits and responsible use

  • A generated IMEI cannot identify a phone, a SIM or a person, and it will not connect to a mobile network.
  • Do not use generated or copied IMEIs to clone, change or spoof a real device's identity, or to get around a block on a lost or stolen phone. Changing a device's IMEI is illegal in many countries, including India and the United States.
  • Do not use these numbers in production systems. Real IMEI data should come from the device or from an authorised source.
